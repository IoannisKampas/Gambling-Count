# Addendum 1 to the implementation brief — four more readings

Apply this on top of the ten-reading brief (`AGENT-BRIEF.md` as you were given it). It adds
**Tetrada, Tetrada 2, Pentada and Pentada 2**, taking the total from ten readings to fourteen.

Nothing already implemented changes behaviour. The reducer is untouched — all four are new rows in
the rules table. But **four statements in the existing brief are now wrong**, and two numbers in it
were wrong when it was written; §2 below lists all of them. Read that section before the vectors.

Every value here was generated by running the reference implementation.

## 2. Corrections to the brief you have

### 2.1 Two rates were wrong

The rarity table in the old brief understated two figures. Recomputed exactly:

| | old brief says | correct |
|---|---|---|
| Triada scoring rate | ≈3.3% | **≈3.4%** |
| Triada 2 scoring rate | ≈2.8% | **≈2.9%** |

Nothing in the rules changes — these were presentation roundings — but if you built a rate check
against them, loosen or correct it.

### 2.2 "Triada 2 is the rarest" is no longer true

The old brief says Triada 2 is the rarest reading. **Pentada 2 is now the rarest.** Replace that
paragraph's table with:

| depth | group-A reading | group-B reading |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monada ≈12.8% | Monada 2 ≈12.2% |
| 2 | Diada ≈6.6% | Diada 2 ≈5.9% |
| 3 | Triada ≈3.4% | Triada 2 ≈2.9% |
| 4 | **Tetrada ≈1.7%** | **Tetrada 2 ≈1.4%** |
| 5 | **Pentada ≈0.89%** | **Pentada 2 ≈0.68%** |

And extend the vs-twin table with:

| | arms vs twin | scores vs twin |
|---|---|---|
| Tetrada 2 | ≈15% less | ≈19% less |
| Pentada 2 | ≈19% less | ≈24% less |

The formula in the old brief still holds: a reading arming at depth *n* arms `(18/19)^(n−1)` as
often as its twin and scores `(18/19)^n` as often. Ena/Duo 2 remains the exception.

### 2.3 The arming-exclusivity property now covers five per group

The old brief's property 6 reads:

> At most one of Monada, Diada, Triada is armed at a time; likewise at most one of Monada 2,
> Diada 2, Triada 2.

Replace with:

> **At most one of Monada, Diada, Triada, Tetrada, Pentada is armed at a time**; likewise at most
> one of their five opposites.

The exception is unchanged and still important: Ena/Duo is armed alongside Monada or Diada by
design, so do **not** extend this to all readings on a group. Ena/Duo is never armed alongside
Triada, Tetrada or Pentada.

### 2.4 The near-miss rule generalises

The old brief lists `ABA` under Diada and `BAAB` under Triada 2 as give-ups that must fire no
event. That is an instance of a general rule, now worth stating outright:

> **A reading arming at depth *n* ignores *n−1* near misses.**

| reading | near misses it must ignore entirely | the only return that resets it |
|---|---|---|
| Monada | — | `ABA` |
| Diada | `ABA` | `ABBA` |
| Triada | `ABA`, `ABBA` | `ABBBA` |
| **Tetrada** | `ABA`, `ABBA`, `ABBBA` | `ABBBBA` |
| **Pentada** | `ABA`, `ABBA`, `ABBBA`, `ABBBBA` | `ABBBBBA` |

Mirrored for the group-B readings (`BAB`, `BAAB`, `BAAAB`, `BAAAAB`; reset on `BAAAAAB` for
Pentada 2). "Ignore entirely" means **no event at all** — not a count, not a reset — and a
standing count must be left exactly where it was.

### 2.5 One new check is required at these depths

Add to the properties list:

> **Observed scoring rates must match the predicted ones** in §2.2 over a long stream.

This matters only for the deep readings, and it matters a lot: an off-by-one in `armAfter` moves
the rate by roughly a factor of two, and for Pentada **no single shape in the vector tables
reveals it**, because every `ABBBBBB`-style shape gives Pentada and Tetrada the same count. The
near-miss rows in §4 are the other way to catch it; the rate check is the backstop.

Use tens of thousands of spins for the Pentada pair. At ~1 count per 112 and 147 spins, a
2,000-spin stream exercises almost no deciders and will pass whatever you do.

## 6. Properties to add

Over seeded pseudo-random streams (tens of thousands of spins for the Pentada pair):

1. **Swap invariance**, as for the existing mirrors: Tetrada 2's count equals Tetrada's on the same
   sequence with every number moved to the other group; likewise Pentada 2 and Pentada.
2. **Neither of a pair ever scores on the same spin as its twin.**
3. **Arming is exact.** Tetrada is armed precisely on the spins where a group-A run has just been
   broken by four Bs, with `originGroup == 'A'`; Pentada at five Bs. Mirrored for the other two.
4. **No single-arm reading on a group is armed alongside another** (now five per group — see §2.3).
5. **No domination in either direction** between any two single-arm readings on the same group.
   Both orderings must actually occur. The deeper reading gets ahead by ignoring near misses that
   wipe the shallower one; the shallower gets ahead by scoring sooner.
6. **Observed rate matches prediction** — see §2.5.

## 7. Using these in practice

Worth passing on to whoever reads the output, because it is easy to mistake for a bug:

**Pentada 2 scores on ≈0.68% of spins — one count every ~147 spins.** On a table turning a spin a
minute that is roughly one count every two and a half hours. Pentada is ≈0.89%, about one every
two hours. At depths 4 and 5:

- A counter sitting at 0 for an entire session is the **expected** reading, not a stalled feed.
  Use the spin stream itself as the liveness signal, not the count.
- Counts rarely chain past −1, because chaining needs the whole six- or seven-spin shape twice over
  with no intervening return.
- Correctness is better judged by the rate check in §2.5 than by watching a live counter, which
  will not produce enough deciders in a session to tell a correct implementation from a wrong one.
